Arthroscopy and sports medicine news for **September 2026**. Studies were found through PubMed (entry date 1–30 September 2026) and the journals *Arthroscopy*, *AJSM*, *KSSTA* and *OJSM*. Every fact has a link. Things I could not check are marked <span class="verify">uncertain</span>.

## 1. Top 5 studies

**1. Young ACL patients: patellar tendon alone vs hamstring + ALL.** The Lyon group (Sonnery-Cottet) published a prespecified subgroup analysis of 281 patients under 25. After 5 years, the graft failed in 16.4% after isolated bone–patellar tendon–bone (BPTB) reconstruction and in 4.8% after hamstring reconstruction with anterolateral ligament (ALL) reconstruction. The risk with isolated BPTB was about four times higher (HR 4.2). Patient-reported scores were the same in both groups. The paper calls itself Level I (secondary analysis of an RCT), but the methods also describe a "prospective comparative study" <span class="verify">uncertain: check the randomisation in the full text</span>. Main message: in young, active patients, adding an anterolateral procedure seems to protect the graft. [KSSTA, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.70617)

**2. BEAR — ACL repair with a bridge, 2 years after market launch.** BEAR (Bridge-Enhanced ACL Restoration) repairs the torn ACL with sutures and a collagen implant soaked in the patient's blood, instead of replacing it with a graft. The first 100 patients in a US registry were followed for about 2 years. Only 5.6% re-tore the ACL. Reoperation was 18.9% overall, but it fell from 28.0% to 7.5% after the surgical technique was modified. Level II, prospective cohort, no control group. [KSSTA, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ksa.70606)

**3. Leukocyte-poor PRP during rotator cuff repair.** A meta-analysis of 5 RCTs (470 patients) found that injecting leukocyte-poor platelet-rich plasma (LP-PRP) during arthroscopic repair almost halved re-tears (15.9% vs 29.0%; RR 0.55). Scores were slightly better, but no difference reached the minimal clinically important difference (MCID). So the tendon heals better on imaging, but the patient may not feel the difference. Level II. [Arthroscopy, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70537)

**4. A shoulder block that spares the diaphragm.** A Chinese non-inferiority RCT (82 patients) compared a new infraspinatus–teres minor interfascial block (ITMB) with the standard interscalene block (ISB) for arthroscopic shoulder surgery. Opioid use in 24 h was the same (about 11 mg oxycodone in both groups). Hemidiaphragmatic paralysis occurred in 2.6% after ITMB vs 90.2% after ISB, and rebound pain in 2.6% vs 26.8%. Level I, single centre. [Drug Des Devel Ther, DOI](https://doi.org/10.2147/DDDT.S624791)

**5. How to space arthroscopy training.** Thirty residents with no arthroscopic experience trained on a knee simulator with breaks of 1 hour, 2 days or 1 week between sessions. One week later, the 1-hour group had lost most of its skill (score 58.9), while the 2-day (87.2) and 1-week (84.8) groups kept it. The 2-day group was also the fastest. Small simulation RCT. [Arthroscopy, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70503)

## 2. China focus

- **Volume.** My PubMed search (arthroscopy / ACL / rotator cuff / meniscus AND China[Affiliation], entry date September 2026) returned **131 records** in one month. Not all are clinical, but it shows the scale.
- **Chinese PLA General Hospital (Beijing).** Started POSTLESS-HIP, a multicentre RCT (270 patients) comparing hip arthroscopy *without* a perineal post vs the classic post. The aim is fewer perineal nerve and soft-tissue injuries. This is a protocol; results will come later. [BMJ Open, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-118402); NCT07322705
- **Peking University Third Hospital (Beijing).** In revision ACL surgery with soft-tissue grafts (104 patients), suture augmentation was linked to fewer graft failures (OR 0.20) and more return to sport (OR 3.85). Level III, retrospective. [Arthroscopy, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70539)
- **AI for knee imaging and planning.** The Second Hospital of Jilin University built KneeFusionNet, a deep learning model that finds ACL, PCL, MCL and LCL injuries on MRI (919 patients, 3 centres; external AUC 0.86–0.89). With AI help, junior surgeons' accuracy rose from 0.818 to 0.900. [Arthroscopy, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70510). Qilu Hospital (Shandong University) made an AI planning system for high tibial osteotomy: about 9 seconds per X-ray instead of 4.3 minutes, with surgeon-level accuracy. [Arthroscopy, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70515)
- **Domestic robots.** A single-centre study (86 patients) compared the Chinese OSR-2000 robot (MicroPort) with manual knee replacement: better alignment and less blood loss, but longer surgery. Retrospective, small. [J Robot Surg, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-026-03969-3)
- **Teaching.** The training-spacing RCT (study 5 above) comes from Zhujiang Hospital, Southern Medical University (Guangzhou).
- **Societies.** The Chinese Society of Sports Medicine held its 2026 congress in Beijing on 26–28 June. [ISAKOS directory](https://isakos.com/Directory/International-Societies-Meetings)

## 3. Techniques & devices

- **Mixed reality in the OR.** Stryker reported the first surgery with *SportSuite Vision* on Apple Vision Pro (Duke Health, hip arthroscopy). It shows the arthroscopic video, 3D navigation and CT images in a headset instead of on monitors. FDA De Novo authorisation. [Orthogate, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.orthogate.org/medtech-innovation/industry/84705-orthopaedic-industry-daily-briefing-2026-09-03)
- **Anchors.** Arthrex received FDA 510(k) clearance (K262357) for its updated bioabsorbable PushLock knotless anchors for labral and soft-tissue repair. [Orthogate, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.orthogate.org/medtech-innovation/industry/84713-orthopaedic-industry-daily-briefing-2026-09-04)
- **Synthetic cartilage.** Nanochon treated the first patient in a first-in-human study of *Chondrograft*, a 3D-printed implant for focal knee cartilage defects. [Orthogate, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.orthogate.org/medtech-innovation/industry/84713-orthopaedic-industry-daily-briefing-2026-09-04)
- **Adjustable-angle arthroscope.** Surgeons from Soochow University tested an arthroscope with a hinged, adjustable viewing angle (instead of fixed 30° or 70°) for drilling the ACL femoral tunnel in 10 pig knees. Early lab study only. [Front Surg, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsurg.2026.1899167)
- **Biology for knee OA.** A meta-analysis of 21 RCTs found that PRP gives *small* but significant improvements in pain and function over hyaluronic acid at 12 months; high-concentration PRP worked better. Heterogeneity was high. [Arthroscopy, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/arj.70520)

## 6. So what for me

1. **Train with breaks, not in one long session.** The spacing RCT suggests that practice every 2 days keeps a skill better than practice with 1-hour breaks. The same idea works for my flashcards and for knot-tying at home.
2. **Learn to read "Level I" carefully.** Studies 1 and 3 show the difference between *what the title says* and *what the methods or MCID show*. A big effect on graft failure or imaging is not always a big effect for the patient.
3. **Follow Chinese centres in English journals.** PLA General Hospital, Peking University Third Hospital and Jilin University publish in *Arthroscopy* and *BMJ Open* every month — good material for reading practice and for watching AI and robots move into sports surgery.
